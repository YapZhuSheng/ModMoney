⚠1 compatibility note
⚠+1" over stock wheel diameter (16" → 17")
Going up in rim size without dropping tire profile raises the overall rolling diameter, which makes the speedometer read low (you will actually be travelling faster than indicated) and adds error to the odometer. To stay near stock rolling diameter on a 17" rim, aim for roughly a 50-series tire (about 185/50 R17). Also budget for the ride-quality loss from the shorter sidewall, and re-check clearance at full steering lock.

ⓘRolling diameter within 1.0% of stock — good match
New setup: 205/45 R17. Overall diameter changes by 6.4 mm (1.0%) versus the stock 185/55 R16. That is inside the ±3% window most people treat as stock-equivalent, so speedometer error stays negligible. Tire profile note: sidewall height is now about 92 mm.
ⓘ45-series tire is low profile
A 45 aspect ratio leaves a short sidewall, so the tire absorbs less of the road. Keep pressures to spec — under-inflation on a low-profile tire concentrates damage on the rim lip.
ⓘTire is 20 mm wider than stock (185 → 205 mm)
Wider rubber adds grip but also adds tramlining, a little more steering effort, and a small fuel-economy penalty. Make sure the rim width suits a 205 mm section, and re-check inner-guard clearance at full lock.

⚠2 compatibility notes
⚠Lowering 35 mm on 17" wheels — check guard clearance
You have "17x7.5 ET42 flow-formed alloys" in this build at 17" (stock is 16"), and this entry lowers the car 35 mm. The combination shrinks the gap between tire and guard from both directions at once, so rubbing on full compression is the most likely problem. Roll the guards or verify clearance with the car fully loaded before signing off on either part.
ⓘLowered 35 mm
Dropping 35 mm reduces suspension travel and steepens camber, which wears the inner shoulder of the tires faster. Get an alignment afterwards. Headlight aim also drops — re-aim after fitting.
Want the gap gone, but not scraping every speed bump.
